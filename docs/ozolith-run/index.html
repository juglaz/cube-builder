Avishkar · Ikoria · The Omenpaths

The race changed when the first crew came home.

When the Omenpaths opened, Avishkar's racing circuit spilled across the Multiverse. Pilots and artificers drove experimental Vehicles through unfamiliar worlds, building for impossible terrain and proving their machines under pressure.

The first run to Ikoria returned with fragments of the Ozolith. Near the crystal, modifications held and compounded: reinforced hulls stayed stronger, creatures kept their adaptations, and salvaged parts carried traces of the machines they had belonged to.

From that moment, the finish line was only half the race. The real prize was the next shard—and every crew wanted to reach it first.

The official face of the Run

Depala's Expedition

Depala, Pilot Exemplar, organized Avishkar's strongest pilots, mechanics, and artificers into an expeditionary motor guild. Their crews chart the routes, breach the ruins, and bring the fragments home under guard.

They call their work recovery, not conquest. Every shard is catalogued. Every Vehicle is purpose-built. Every upgrade is tested on the track before it is trusted in the field.

But the distinction grows thinner with every victory. The more fragments Depala secures, the stronger her fleet becomes—and the more dangerous it would be to surrender them.

Build the machine that survives the route. Then build the route that only your machine can survive.

The race becomes a robbery

The Salvage Cartel

The Cartel does not waste time mapping ruins or surviving their traps. Its crews follow the people who do.

A convoy can spend weeks reaching a fragment and lose it in seconds on the return run. Raiders strike at speed, cut loose the cargo, and disappear through unofficial Omenpaths before Depala's heavier Vehicles can turn. None is more infamous than Smuggler's Copter.

When a Vehicle goes down, the Cartel turns the wreck into a second race. Engines, armor, cargo, and crystal belong to whoever reaches them first. What cannot be sold is stripped. What cannot be stripped is burned.

Let the expedition find the prize. The open road will make it ours.

Nothing useful stays dead

Greasefang's Gang

Greasefang and her gang came through a Kamigawan Omenpath with a simpler rule: a Vehicle is not dead because it hit the wreckfield. It is dead only when nobody can make it move.

Her mechanics winch broken racers out of the dirt, hammer their engines awake, and throw them back onto the track before the dust settles. The repairs do not need to last. They need to hold through one more ambush, one more straightaway, or one final sprint.

Unlike the Cartel, Greasefang's crews do not see wreckage as inventory. They see unfinished races. A chassis abandoned by Depala or gutted by smugglers may return hours later under Greasefang's colors—faster, meaner, and carrying every old scar.

If it crossed the finish line once, it can cross it again.

The fragments remember each other

The race is rebuilding the Ozolith.

Shards stored together begin to align. Distant machines develop the same unfamiliar modifications. Navigation instruments pull toward fragments no crew has found. Then one shard moves on its own—toward another.

The Ozolith did not simply break. Its pieces are trying to reconnect, and every faction has become part of the mechanism.

Depala gathers themto protect them and power the expedition.
The Cartel gathers themto concentrate their value and sell them.
Greasefang returns theminside wrecks that should have stayed buried.

Everyone believes they are chasing the Ozolith. The Run may be how the Ozolith is finding itself.

The outcome is never fixed

Every draft is another stretch of the Run.

Depala may secure the fragments. The Cartel may steal them. Greasefang may put a dead fleet back on the track. The cards decide who wins this leg—and what the Ozolith becomes when the dust clears.
